This paper examines the structural sacrifices that capitalist systems demand of citizens -- not only in labor and wages, but in agency, time, health, and the capacity to participate in public life on terms that are not dictated by market logic.

The argument connects to the broader writing record on political economy and the conditions of survival under systems that treat stability as success even when people inside them are deteriorating.
